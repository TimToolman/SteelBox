Three things set the date — none of them are engineering

Every one of these is an application to somebody else, and each has a queue we can't jump. They are filed in week 1 so they mature while the code is being written, not after.

Days → weeks

Stripe underwriting

Business verification before the account can take live charges. Nothing in P3 reaches production until it clears.

2 → 6 weeks

10DLC registration

Brand and campaign approval before a single marketing text sends legally. Filed week 1, used week 9.

Weeks, plus review

Legal documents

Terms of sale, rental agreement, privacy policy, refund policy, insurance. You cannot charge a card without them.

The Aarcadian track

Aarcadian is the supply side of this business — containers, consignment, and the damage economics that turn a written-off box into a listed one. Almost none of it is engineering; most of it is two teams and a lawyer, which is why it starts in week 1 and why the estimates below are calendar, not effort.

What would move week 9

  • !Stripe verification stalls. Only fix is filing in week 1 and answering same-day. Watched weekly from day one.
  • !Legal review runs long. Checkout cannot go live without a refund policy on the page — this is the likeliest slip.
  • !Postgres migration overruns. Everything downstream is behind it; it is deliberately first and alone.
  • !10DLC rejection. Costs weeks, but hits SMS in week 9 — not the retail launch itself.
  • !Aarcadian contracts slip. Retail launch does not depend on them — but the New Orleans reseller's inventory does, and week 8 is already tight for counsel on three schedules.
  • !Scope added at the door. Rentals, multi-tenancy and SMS are already sequenced after launch. Keeping them there is what protects the date.
